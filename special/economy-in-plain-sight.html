03Peer-reviewed

Oil tank shadows

A floating tank roof sinks as oil drains. Satellites measure the shadow inside the rim to work out how full the tank is.

Fewer joltsin oil prices when the US published its weekly stock figures, once satellite estimates existed.
What it seesHow full the tanks are.
What it missesCloudy weeks, fixed roofs.

Mukherjee, Panayotov and Shon, Journal of Financial Economics, 2021.

Illustration: KKReckons. Each ping gives a ship's position; strung together they show a voyage.

04Working paper

Ship pings

Every large ship broadcasts its position. The IMF turned those radio signals into a live map of world trade.

80%+of goods trade by volume moves by sea. The pings sometimes arrive before customs data.
What it seesShip movements, port calls.
What it missesCargo contents and value.

Cerdeiro, Komaromi, Liu and Saeed, IMF Working Paper 20/57, 2020.

Signal or noise?

Which signals have earned trust

1. Night lights

8.6 / 10 · strong
Peer-reviewedMonthlyGood for: Now, with some lead

Tested in a top journal and again by RBI staff on unseen quarters. Free from NASA, with images back to 1992. Sees: Change in lit-up activity, anywhere on Earth. Misses: Farms, offices and anything that makes little light.

2. Power, rail and fuel use

7.5 / 10 · strong
Institutional researchWeeklyGood for: Now

The Fed's Weekly Economic Index blends ten weekly series like these and is scaled to yearly GDP growth. It started in April 2020 and covers the US only. Sees: Physical activity: electricity load, rail traffic, fuel sales. Misses: Services, prices and anything driven by the weather.

3. Ship tracking

7.3 / 10 · strong
Institutional researchDailyGood for: Now, sometimes days ahead

IMF papers show a good match with official trade for many countries. The record only starts around 2015. Sees: Where cargo ships are and how full ports look. Misses: What is in the containers and what it is worth.

4. Google searches

6.8 / 10 · middling
Peer-reviewed, mixedDailyGood for: Now only

Free, fast and global, which lifts the score. The evidence itself is mixed: one famous success, one famous failure. Sees: What people are worried about or shopping for. Misses: Whether they act on it. Results shift as Google changes.

5. Job postings

6.7 / 10 · middling
Industry dataDailyGood for: Now, leads hiring a little

Indeed's index tracks official job openings closely and is free to download, but the series is short. Sees: Employers' demand for new staff, daily. Misses: Jobs filled without an advert, and one website's share of the market.

6. Card spending

6.0 / 10 · middling
Working paperWeeklyGood for: Now

Strong match with official retail data, but most of the raw data is private. Sees: What households spend, by place and income group. Misses: Cash, and spending outside the card networks.

7. Car parks from space

5.7 / 10 · middling
Peer-reviewedWeeklyGood for: Now, days before earnings

The best-proven investor signal here, in two journals. It costs institutional money and covers US retailers only. Sees: Estimated visits to physical stores. Misses: Online orders and how much each shopper spends.

8. Oil tank images

5.3 / 10 · middling
Peer-reviewedWeeklyGood for: Now

A journal study shows markets react less to official stock figures once satellites exist. The data is commercial. Sees: How full floating-roof oil tanks are. Misses: Tanks under cloud, with fixed roofs or underground.

9. Phone footfall

4.0 / 10 · weak
Industry estimateWeeklyGood for: Now

Vendors publish plenty, independent tests are few. Sees: Estimated visits, dwell time and repeat trips. Misses: Sales. The panel also skews towards some phone users.

10. Social media mood

3.6 / 10 · weak
KKReckons judgementDailyGood for: Unproven

Fast and global, but we found no solid evidence it tracks the economy. Sees: What is being talked about right now. Misses: Bots, media cycles and everyone who is not posting.

11. Cardboard boxes

3.2 / 10 · weak
Industry dataSlowerGood for: Unproven

A long industry record and a good story, with no peer-reviewed forecasting test. Sees: Goods being packed and shipped. Misses: Mailers and bags replacing boxes as shopping moves online.

12. Sewage chemistry

2.9 / 10 · weak
Peer-reviewed for healthMonthlyGood for: Unproven for the economy

Proven for drug and health monitoring. As an economic signal it is still an experiment. Sees: What a city eats, drinks and takes. Misses: Output, income or spending.

The data that lied

Google Flu Trends: the clever idea that drifted

The promise

Searches for flu symptoms would reveal an outbreak faster than official counts that take weeks.

The failure

The tracker kept running above what doctors actually recorded, at one point by more than double.

100 / 108

One square per week from August 2011 to September 2013. Filled: the tracker ran too high. The squares show the count, not the order of the weeks.

  1. 2008Google launches the tracker.
  2. 2009It misses the out-of-season swine flu pandemic. Google rebuilds it.
  3. 2011The long run of overestimates begins.
  4. 2013Nature reports it predicting more than double the CDC's figure.
  5. 2014Researchers find it was partly a winter detector. Combined with CDC data, it beat either alone.

Peer-reviewed Lazer, Kennedy, King and Vespignani, Science, 2014.

The other side

Three more reasons for caution

  • An edge everyone has is no edge

    Once satellite car counts went on sale, lenders who had relied on private knowledge of borrowers pulled back from loans. The more funds buy a dataset, the less it is worth.

    Kang, Journal of Accounting and Economics, 2024 · peer-reviewed

  • Prices are the blind spot

    When the 2025 US shutdown stopped official releases, private data covered jobs far better than inflation. Alternative data sees activity well and prices poorly.

    Chicago Fed; American Action Forum · institutional research and commentary

  • Some signals are only stories

    Cardboard box shipments are a favourite recession signal, and counting workers' cars at factories sounds clever. We found no peer-reviewed test of either.

    Fibre Box Association data via industry reports; Virginia Tech · KKReckons judgement

Is US hiring picking up?
  1. Open Indeed Hiring Lab and pick the United States.
  2. See which way postings have moved in three months.
  3. Open jobless claims on FRED.
  4. If postings rise and claims stay low, both point the same way. If they disagree, wait for more data.
Are goods moving faster in India?
  1. Find this month's e-way bill count in the GST release.
  2. Compare it with the same month last year, not last month.
  3. Check whether a festival moved between the two months.
  4. Look at UPI too. If only one is up, be cautious.
Words used in this report
Nowcasting Estimating what the economy is doing right now, before the official number is out.
Alternative data Data made for another purpose, such as payments or satellite images, used to read the economy.
Proxy Something you can measure that stands in for something you cannot.
Unseen-data test Checking a model on periods it was not built on. Also called out-of-sample testing.
RMSE A model's typical error. Smaller is better.
Correlation How closely two things move together, from 0 to 1. It does not show that one causes the other.
Leading and coincident A leading signal turns before the economy does. A coincident one moves with it.
Working paper Research published before a journal's reviewers have checked it.
The takeaway

Visits are not sales, lights are not income and searches are not jobs. This data shows you now, not next.
