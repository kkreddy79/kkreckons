The economy hides in plain sight. Who spots it first?

Satellites, shopping carts, search bars and shipping containers often show where the economy is weeks before the official numbers arrive. Here is what works, what flops and what you can check yourself.

By KK Reddy · Sources: peer-reviewed studies, RBI, IMF and Federal Reserve research · Research to 21 November 2026

A window, not a crystal ball. Alternative data can reveal the economy today, sometimes before official statistics arrive. Predicting tomorrow is a different challenge. The four clues above are four different kinds of evidence, so they are not directly comparable.
Six strange places to look

Lights, car parks, shadows, pings, searches and swipes

Each card has the finding, what the signal sees, what it misses and the study behind it.

Night lights

🛰️

Brighter nights mean more activity, so economists used satellite images to check official growth figures.

Official 8.6%
Lights 3.4%
Myanmar, average yearly GDP growth, 1992 to 2003, to scale. In Congo it ran the other way: lights +2.4%, official −2.6%.
What it seesChange in lit-up activity.
What it missesDim sectors: farms, offices.

Peer-reviewed Henderson, Storeygard and Weil, American Economic Review, 2012. Official figures: World Bank.

Car parks

🅿️

Satellites counted cars outside 44 US retailers. Traders bought the fullest quarter and bet against the emptiest.

Filling +1.6%
Emptying −3.0%
Share move against the market in the three days around quarterly earnings, 2011 to 2017. Bars to scale.
What it seesEstimated store visits.
What it missesOnline orders, basket size.

Peer-reviewed Katona, Painter, Patatoukas and Zeng, JFQA, 2025. Data: RS Metrics.

Oil tank shadows

🛢️

A floating tank roof sinks as oil drains, and the shadow inside the rim grows. Satellites measure the shadow.

Full: thin shadow
Drained: wide shadow
Diagram, not data. Once such estimates existed, oil prices jumped less when the US published its weekly stock figures.
What it seesHow full the tanks are.
What it missesCloudy weeks, fixed roofs.

Peer-reviewed Mukherjee, Panayotov and Shon, Journal of Financial Economics, 2021.

Ship pings

🚢

Every large ship broadcasts its position. The IMF turned those radio signals into a live map of world trade.

More than 80% of goods trade by volume moves by sea. The pings sometimes arrive before customs data.
What it seesShip movements, port calls.
What it missesCargo contents and value.

Working paper Cerdeiro, Komaromi, Liu and Saeed, IMF Working Paper 20/57, 2020.

Search bars

🔎

People search for jobs and unemployment benefits before they show up in the claims figures.

Without
With searches
Average error when estimating US jobless claims: 15.7% smaller once searches were added. Bars to scale.
What it seesWhat people look for.
What it missesWhether they act on it.

Peer-reviewed Choi and Varian, Economic Record, 2012. A 2018 re-run found weaker gains.

Card swipes

💳

Card data showed who stopped spending in 2020 within weeks, long before official figures could.

More than half of the fall in US card spending by 10 June 2020 came from high-income households. The bar shows the half-way mark, not an exact share.
What it seesSpending by place, income.
What it missesCash and off-network spending.

Working paper Chetty, Friedman, Hendren, Stepner and the Opportunity Insights team, NBER, 2020.

Signal or noise?

Which signals have earned trust

Twelve signals, placed by how strong the evidence is and how fast the data arrives. Tap a number.

1. Night lights

8.6 / 10 · strong
Peer-reviewedMonthlyGood for: Now, with some lead

Tested in a top journal and again by RBI staff on unseen quarters. Free from NASA, with images back to 1992. Sees: Change in lit-up activity, anywhere on Earth. Misses: Farms, offices and anything that makes little light.

2. Power, rail and fuel use

7.5 / 10 · strong
Institutional researchWeeklyGood for: Now

The Fed's Weekly Economic Index blends ten weekly series like these and is scaled to yearly GDP growth. It started in April 2020 and covers the US only. Sees: Physical activity: electricity load, rail traffic, fuel sales. Misses: Services, prices and anything driven by the weather.

3. Ship tracking

7.3 / 10 · strong
Institutional researchDailyGood for: Now, sometimes days ahead

IMF papers show a good match with official trade for many countries. The record only starts around 2015. Sees: Where cargo ships are and how full ports look. Misses: What is in the containers and what it is worth.

4. Google searches

6.8 / 10 · middling
Peer-reviewed, mixedDailyGood for: Now only

Free, fast and global, which lifts the score. The evidence itself is mixed: one famous success, one famous failure. Sees: What people are worried about or shopping for. Misses: Whether they act on it. Results shift as Google changes.

5. Job postings

6.7 / 10 · middling
Industry dataDailyGood for: Now, leads hiring a little

Indeed's index tracks official job openings closely and is free to download, but the series is short. Sees: Employers' demand for new staff, daily. Misses: Jobs filled without an advert, and one website's share of the market.

6. Card spending

6.0 / 10 · middling
Working paperWeeklyGood for: Now

Strong match with official retail data, but most of the raw data is private. Sees: What households spend, by place and income group. Misses: Cash, and spending outside the card networks.

7. Car parks from space

5.7 / 10 · middling
Peer-reviewedWeeklyGood for: Now, days before earnings

The best-proven investor signal here, in two journals. It costs institutional money and covers US retailers only. Sees: Estimated visits to physical stores. Misses: Online orders and how much each shopper spends.

8. Oil tank images

5.3 / 10 · middling
Peer-reviewedWeeklyGood for: Now

A journal study shows markets react less to official stock figures once satellites exist. The data is commercial. Sees: How full floating-roof oil tanks are. Misses: Tanks under cloud, with fixed roofs or underground.

9. Phone footfall

4.0 / 10 · weak
Industry estimateWeeklyGood for: Now

Vendors publish plenty, independent tests are few. Sees: Estimated visits, dwell time and repeat trips. Misses: Sales. The panel also skews towards some phone users.

10. Social media mood

3.6 / 10 · weak
KKReckons judgementDailyGood for: Unproven

Fast and global, but we found no solid evidence it tracks the economy. Sees: What is being talked about right now. Misses: Bots, media cycles and everyone who is not posting.

11. Cardboard boxes

3.2 / 10 · weak
Industry dataSlowerGood for: Unproven

A long industry record and a good story, with no peer-reviewed forecasting test. Sees: Goods being packed and shipped. Misses: Mailers and bags replacing boxes as shopping moves online.

12. Sewage chemistry

2.9 / 10 · weak
Peer-reviewed for healthMonthlyGood for: Unproven for the economy

Proven for drug and health monitoring. As an economic signal it is still an experiment. Sees: What a city eats, drinks and takes. Misses: Output, income or spending.

The other side

Where the clever data went wrong

The data that lied · Google Flu Trends
  1. The promise2008Google launches a flu tracker built on search terms, faster than official counts that take weeks.
  2. First miss2009It misses the swine flu pandemic, which struck out of season. Google rebuilds the model.
  3. The drift2011–13From August 2011 it runs too high in 100 of 108 weeks.
  4. The headline2013Nature reports it predicting more than double the doctor visits the CDC recorded.
  5. The lesson2014Researchers find it was partly a winter detector. Combined with CDC data, it beat either alone.

Peer-reviewed Lazer, Kennedy, King and Vespignani, Science, 2014. The 100 of 108 weeks covers August 2011 to September 2013, not the tracker's whole life.

An edge everyone has is no edge

Once satellite car counts went on sale, lenders who had relied on private knowledge of borrowers pulled back from loans. The more funds buy a dataset, the less it is worth.

Kang, Journal of Accounting and Economics, 2024 · peer-reviewed

Prices are the blind spot

When the 2025 US shutdown stopped official releases, private data covered jobs far better than inflation. Alternative data sees activity well and prices poorly.

Chicago Fed; American Action Forum · institutional research and commentary

The cardboard box legend

Box shipments are a favourite recession signal, and US third-quarter shipments in 2025 were the lowest since 2015. But no peer-reviewed study shows boxes forecasting anything.

Fibre Box Association data via industry reports; Virginia Tech · industry data

Some signals are only stories

Counting workers' cars at factories sounds clever. We found no published study that tests it, so it is not on our chart.

Try it yourself

Six free windows on the economy

No terminal, no subscription.

  • FREDSt. Louis Fed · weekly and monthly · no sign-upTry: chart weekly jobless claims back to 2020.
  • Indeed Hiring LabDaily data, refreshed weekly · no sign-upTry: compare job postings in two countries.
  • Google TrendsNear real time · no sign-upTry: searches for "jobs" in your state over five years.
  • NPCIMonthly · no sign-upTry: UPI volume this month against a year ago.
  • IMF PortWatchDaily ship data · no sign-upTry: ships through Suez this week against last year.
  • NASA Black MarbleDaily, monthly, yearly since 2012 · free NASA login to downloadTry: find your city in the yearly night-lights image.
Is US hiring picking up?
  1. Open Indeed Hiring Lab and pick the United States.
  2. See which way postings have moved in three months.
  3. Open jobless claims on FRED.
  4. If postings rise and claims stay low, both point the same way. If they disagree, wait for more data.
Are goods moving faster in India?
  1. Find this month's e-way bill count in the GST release.
  2. Compare it with the same month last year, not last month.
  3. Check whether a festival moved between the two months.
  4. Look at UPI too. If only one is up, be cautious.
Words used in this report
Nowcasting Estimating what the economy is doing right now, before the official number is out.
Alternative data Data made for another purpose, such as payments or satellite images, used to read the economy.
Proxy Something you can measure that stands in for something you cannot.
Unseen-data test Checking a model on periods it was not built on. Also called out-of-sample testing.
RMSE A model's typical error. Smaller is better.
Correlation How closely two things move together, from 0 to 1. It does not show that one causes the other.
Leading and coincident A leading signal turns before the economy does. A coincident one moves with it.
Working paper Research published before a journal's reviewers have checked it.
The takeaway

Visits are not sales, lights are not income and searches are not jobs. This data shows you now, not next.

  1. 1A proxy is not the thing itself.
  2. 2Moving together is not cause and effect.
  3. 3Trust a signal only after it has been tested against what actually happened.
