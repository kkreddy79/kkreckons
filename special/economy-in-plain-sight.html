The economy hides in plain sight. Who spots it first?

Satellites, shopping carts, search bars and shipping containers often show where the economy is weeks before the official numbers arrive. Here is what works, what flops and what you can check yourself.

By KK Reddy · Sources: peer-reviewed studies, RBI, IMF and Federal Reserve research · Research to 21 November 2026

Six strange places to look

Lights, car parks, shadows, pings, searches and swipes

Each one is a real study with a real result, and a catch.

Night lights

🛰️

Brighter nights mean more activity. Economists used satellite images to check official growth figures.

Official 8.6%
Lights 3.4%
Myanmar, to scale. In Congo it ran the other way: lights said +2.4% a year, official data −2.6%.

Catch Farms and offices are dim, and new power lines add light without adding output.

Car parks

🅿️

Satellites counted cars outside 44 US retailers from 2011 to 2017, quarter after quarter.

+1.6%three-day gain around earnings when lots were filling; nearly −3% when they were emptying

Catch Short sellers took most of the gain, and online orders never park.

Oil tank shadows

🛢️

A floating tank roof sinks as oil drains, and its shadow grows. Satellites measure the shadow.

Fewer joltsin oil prices when the US published its weekly stock figures, once satellite estimates existed

Catch In cloudy weeks the satellites saw nothing, and the surprises came back.

Ship pings

🚢

Every large ship broadcasts its position. The IMF turned those radio signals into a live map of trade.

80%+of goods trade by volume moves by sea, and the pings sometimes arrive before customs data

Catch The cargo's weight is a guess from how low the ship sits. A container's contents stay hidden.

Search bars

🔎

People search for "jobs" and "unemployment benefits" before they show up in the claims figures.

15.7%smaller error in estimating US jobless claims when Google searches were added

Catch A 2018 re-run found the gain depends on the country and the years tested.

Card swipes

💳

Card data showed who stopped spending in 2020 within weeks, long before official figures could.

Over halfof the fall in US card spending by June 2020 came from high-income households

Catch Cash and anything outside the card networks is invisible.

India from orbit

Three states, measured by lamplight

India publishes state output once a year, about 12 to 18 months late. In October 2026, RBI staff tried night lights plus Google searches instead.

Read it carefully. This is research by RBI staff, not RBI policy. It estimates the current quarter, not the future, covers three states only, and is tested against a quarterly series the authors had to build from annual data.

Phones that visit a place leave an anonymous trace. Add them up and you get footfall for every store of a chain that publishes no sales at all.

What it can't tell you

🤔

A visit is not a sale. One car at the drive-thru may be one burger or six.

The winner depends on the rules. This ranking left out a Glendale store beside a mall. A different data provider's page puts that store first.

Nobody can check it. In-N-Out is private, so there are no reported sales to compare against.

The other side

Where the clever data went wrong

The flu tracker that cried wolf

Google Flu Trends overestimated US flu in 100 of 108 weeks from August 2011. It had matched 50 million search terms to a small set of data points and partly learned to spot winter.

Lazer and others, Science, 2014

An edge everyone has is no edge

Once satellite car counts went on sale, lenders who had relied on private knowledge of borrowers pulled back from loans. The more funds buy a dataset, the less it is worth.

Kang, Journal of Accounting and Economics, 2024

Prices are the blind spot

When the 2025 US shutdown stopped official releases, private data covered jobs far better than inflation. Alternative data sees activity well and prices poorly.

Chicago Fed; American Action Forum

The cardboard box legend

Box shipments are a favourite recession signal, and US third-quarter shipments in 2025 were the lowest since 2015. But no peer-reviewed study shows boxes forecasting anything.

Fibre Box Association data via industry reports; Virginia Tech

Estimates dressed as counts

Footfall figures are scaled up from a panel of phones. A Placer.ai executive has said plainly that they are estimates, not sales data.

Store Brands interview

Some signals are only stories

Counting workers' cars at factories sounds clever. We found no published study that tests it, so it stays out of our scoreboard.

Six free windows on the economy

No terminal, no subscription.

  • Indeed Hiring LabA daily job postings index, free to download.
  • Google TrendsSearch interest for any phrase, by country or state.

  • IMF PortWatchShip traffic through ports, Suez and Panama.
  • NASA Black MarbleThe night-lights data RBI staff used.
The takeaway

Visits are not sales, lights are not income and searches are not jobs. This data shows you now, not next.
